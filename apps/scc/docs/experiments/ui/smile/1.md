# UI Smile 1 — airport feedback field

- **Route:** `/ui/smile/1`
- **Date:** 2026-09-04
- **Baseline:** public-facility, single-press satisfaction buttons rather than
  platform emoji or a branded kiosk recreation.

## Trial contract

1. **Participant situation:** a traveller meets a familiar, wordless
   satisfaction control immediately after a public-facility encounter.
2. **Primary parameter:** the local propagation of satisfied, neutral, and
   dissatisfied responses across a responsive field.
3. **Perceptual job:** preserve the one-second recognition of three circular
   feedback buttons while their boundary conditions spread through the field.
4. **Interaction job:** every circle remains a real, keyboard-focusable button
   with quiet press feedback. It makes no network request, persists no response,
   and claims no aggregation or service workflow.
5. **Wrapper justification:** an even field turns an ordinarily isolated exit
   control into the material under examination. The field is not a dashboard,
   terminal reconstruction, or simulated facility.
6. **System family:** `ui/smile` holds only its own addressable variant;
   no shared visual abstraction can silently revise another UI trial.
7. **Removal test:** titles, prompts, brand marks, counters, controls, cards,
   and borders were removed. The faces, their order, and equal grid spacing are
   the complete proposition.

## Preserved invariants

- The field meets every viewport edge with no outer inset. A fixed 8px gap is
  identical in both directions.
- The `ResizeObserver` measures the rendered circle, not an assumed viewport,
  and recalculates a bounded number of cells from the live inner field. The
  ceiling-based capacity deliberately overfills and crops at the edges, as in
  the retained `ui/buttons/1` field.
- Each cell's chance of advancing to its successor is `0.46 × (successor
  neighbours / 8)`, evaluated against all eight wrapped neighbours. A fixed
  per-cell successor mutation rate of `0.004` is part of the same rule, so a
  uniform field is not an absorbing state. The deterministic pseudo-random
  sequence makes the trial reproducible; there is no stall detection or
  conditional reseeding. The field advances once every 650ms, allowing its
  500ms mouth morph to reach the exact next state before another generation,
  and is motionless
  when reduced motion is requested.
- The faces are local SVG geometry, avoiding platform-specific emoji glyphs or
  a vendor's registered terminal artwork. Faces distinguish states without
  relying only on colour.
- All three use the same restrained black line weight: the mouth curve, not a
  traffic-light palette, carries the response.
- Every state has a complete Bézier mouth path with identical command
  topology. One shared animation-frame scheduler interpolates only changed
  cells' SVG `d` coordinates from the previous path to the next; unchanged
  cells are memoized and the transition does not alter layout or icon bounds.

## Reference note

Schiphol reports installing FeedbackNow voting points in airport toilets and
using their live votes to change cleaning schedules; its published image shows
the three-button green, yellow, and red arrangement. Kraków Airport describes
using Smiley Terminals at restrooms and other passenger touchpoints. The local
trial uses the generic public-interface grammar, not either supplier's product
design or name. [Schiphol: *A year's worth of feedback on our toilets*](https://www.schiphol.nl/en/innovation/blog/a-years-worth-of-feedback-on-our-toilets/)
and [HappyOrNot: *Krakow Airport*](https://www.happy-or-not.com/en/insights/customer-story/krakow-airport-on-managing-cx-in-the-next-normal/).
