# Artificial language experiment

Route: `/artificial-language/1`, owned by the filesystem-only
`complex-systems` group.

Date: 2026-08-13.

## Interface premise

1. **Participant situation:** one person encounters a live field of speakers with no central dictionary and can make a particular meaning enter the next exchange.
2. **Primary parameter:** the distributed mapping between six familiar meanings and invented word forms.
3. **Perceptual job:** see a form travel over a social tie, settle through repeated agreement, be copied after a failed exchange, or split through a local mutation.
4. **Interaction job:** choose “speak water,” “speak fire,” and the other meanings to force an immediate utterance, then compare the competing forms and their current number of speakers.
5. **Wrapper justification:** the field makes words—not nodes or charts—the visible material moving between local speakers. A word is drawn both beside the speaker who currently prefers it and while it crosses a relation.
6. **System family:** the complex-systems mineral ground, charcoal relations, blue-grey stable transmission, muted-rust mutation, serif word forms, monospaced controls, and full-viewport direct encounter.
7. **Removal test:** the speaker field, word transmission, visible lexical competition, meaning actions, pause, and new-field action remain. A legend, system dashboard, metrics panel, and simulated connection state are omitted.

## Bounded trial

- **Baseline:** the full-viewport `complex-systems` simulation grammar established by `living-topology` and `cellular-automata`.
- **Changed variable:** the evolving entity is a shared vocabulary. Twenty-one agents begin in three local communities, each with its own forms for six meanings; ties join near neighbours and a small number of cross-community contacts.
- **Local rules:** a tie is chosen in proportion to its accrued affinity. If listener and speaker share a form, both the lexical association and their tie strengthen. Otherwise the listener copies the form, except when weak affinity permits a one-character phonetic mutation. Copying keeps a weakened alternate, so local polysemy can persist and later compete.
- **Retained invariants:** no central interpreter, no global scoring rule selecting a winner, stable agent IDs and positions, explicit rule-level controls, one visual plane, and no socket dependency.
- **Observable result:** not yet browser-observed in this implementation pass. The model explicitly records agreement, adoption, mutation, lexical strength, and relation affinity; its screen maps only those state changes to words, marks, and transmission paths.
- **Unresolved question:** should a later variant let semantic meanings themselves split or merge, rather than only allowing their pronunciations to compete?
