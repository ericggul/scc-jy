# Hypertext network experiment

Route: `/hypertext-network/1`, owned by the filesystem-only
`complex-systems` group.

Date: 2026-08-13.

## Interface premise

1. **Participant situation:** one reader encounters an already active field of
   short page fragments and can leave a further fragment directly in that
   field.
2. **Primary parameter:** the evolving relation between fragments: link
   strength, reader traffic, local attention, and the appearance or loss of
   pages.
3. **Perceptual job:** distinguish a visited relation from a fading one, and
   see that page wording, adjacency, and even the collection of pages are
   changed by the network's reading activity.
4. **Interaction job:** press the field to insert a marginal page, anticipate
   connections to nearby fragments, and then observe whether readers preserve,
   rewrite, expand, or let that relation fade.
5. **Wrapper justification:** hypertext is encountered as linked writing, so
   small textual fragments—not generic nodes—are the material of the field.
   The link strokes and travelling readers make movement between passages
   perceptible without converting the work into a diagram dashboard.
6. **System family:** mineral ground, charcoal writing and relations, blue-grey
   reader activity, muted rust participant trace, serif title, monospaced
   fragments and controls, and one uninterrupted field.
7. **Removal test:** the fragments, links, travelling readers, direct insertion,
   pause/reset controls, and compact counts remain. Cards, legends, simulated
   live status, analytics charts, and explanatory captions do not help a
   participant read or alter the network and are omitted.

## Bounded trial

- **Baseline:** the full-viewport complex-systems simulation grammar established
  by `living-topology/1`.
- **Changed variable:** topology is now made from readable hypertext fragments.
  Each reader travels a link, reinforces the arrival and relation, and chooses a
  subsequent link. Periodic local changes bud a related fragment, prune a weak
  relation, form a compatible relation, or rewrite a passage using a neighbour's
  term.
- **Retained invariants:** stable model-layer IDs, direct manipulation, a
  non-scrollable viewport, local browser simulation without sockets, and no
  decorative dashboard wrapper.
- **Observed result:** implementation complete; browser observation remains a
  separate step. The model does not claim to reproduce the Web's real ranking,
  authoring, or readership systems; its “readers” are bounded simulated agents.
- **Unresolved question:** should a later fork let user-inserted fragments retain
  authored text rather than drawing words from the simulation's limited local
  vocabulary?
