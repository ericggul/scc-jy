# Mycorrhizal wave experiments

Routes: `/mycorrhizal-wave/1` and `/mycorrhizal-wave/2`.

Source: L. Oyarte Galvez et al., “A travelling-wave strategy for plant–fungal
trade,” *Nature* 639, 172–180 (2025),
[doi:10.1038/s41586-025-08614-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08614-x),
and the authors' replication package,
[doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.27889143](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.27889143).

The family is separate from `living-topology`: an anastomosing fungal network
is not a tree, and these trials inspect one published biological system rather
than proposing a general topology primitive.

## 1 — plate 94

- **Baseline:** `/living-topology/6`, retained as the point where the paper-led
  experiment left the earlier generic topology family.
- **Changed variable:** only route ownership and source organization change.
  The observed plate 94 skeleton and event-node timeline remain identical.
- **Participant task:** play, pause, and scrub one measured network while
  comparing tip, branch, and anastomosis events to Fig. 1b.
- **Invariants:** white field, black skeleton, red/orange/green event semantics,
  root-side baseline, physical scale, and observed coordinates.

## 2 — seeded mechanistic travelling-wave simulation

Extended Data Fig. 8e is the visual and temporal comparison target, not an
image source. Every visible filament in this trial is generated by a causal,
seeded network-growth model. The three rows share one rule set and differ only
in their random seed; the columns expose each generated history at 0 h, 30 h,
and 60 h plus a shared scrubbed offset.

- **State:** explicit nodes, active edges, and growing tips. Each tip stores its
  current node, direction, age, type, lifespan, and previous edge.
- **Transition:** at each 0.5 h step, a tip senses local filament density and
  may extend, branch, stop, leave the field, or fuse with an existing segment.
  A fusion creates a node at the collision, retires the collided edge, splits
  it into two edges, and closes the incoming connection. The result is an
  evolving graph with cycles, not a polyline animation.
- **Branch types:** persistent runner hyphae and shorter absorbing branches.
  Their nominal branch angles are 73° and 80° respectively.
- **BAS morphology:** absorbing tips use a higher local recursive branching
  hazard and finite 6–16 h lifespans, producing short dendritic absorbing
  structures behind the runner front. These morphology settings are explicit
  graph-level assumptions; they are not parameters of the continuum BARE PDE.
- **Paper calibration:** runner speed 0.28 mm h⁻¹; branching coefficient
  0.039 h⁻¹; branch-density attenuation 0.007; fusion coefficient 0.022; and
  stopping coefficient 0.009. These are translated into finite-step hazards
  while spatial collision supplies the local fusion condition.
- **Initial condition:** five root-side runner tips and a 12 h warm-up create a
  non-empty `t = 0 h` state. This is a modelling assumption, not a recovered
  experimental initial condition.
- **Replicates:** row labels retain the paper notebook's 822, 918, and 866
  identifiers only as deterministic seed mnemonics. The simulation does not
  reconstruct those plates' exact geometry and does not read their videos.
- **Treatment boundary:** because Extended Data Fig. 8 reports no detected
  travelling-wave difference between sucrose and no-sucrose conditions, the
  trial does not invent a sucrose-dependent rule. All rows use the same model.
- **Validation invariants:** generated histories must be deterministic per
  seed, topological and metric growth must occur between 0 h and 60 h, at least
  one anastomosis must close a cycle, and different seeds must yield different
  geometries. Pure model tests enforce these properties.
- **Presentation invariant:** the paper's nine-frame comparison, physical
  aspect ratio, time placement, and 1 mm scale are retained so simulated
  histories can be judged against Fig. 8e without becoming a dashboard.

This is a mechanistic realization calibrated to reported rates, not a claim of
unique parameter inference. The paper's continuum BARE equations constrain
population-scale tip and filament-density dynamics but do not uniquely specify
microscopic graph geometry; the collision rule and warm-up are therefore
explicit model extensions that make a node–edge simulation possible.
