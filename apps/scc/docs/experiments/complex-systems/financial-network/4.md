# Financial network / 4 — transaction-only field

Route: `/financial-network/4`.

## Tested relation

This is an independent fork of `/financial-network/3`. It tests one changed
relation: whether the existing stock-flow economy is easier to perceive as a
live market when an edge means an actual current transfer, rather than a
permanent account relationship.

The `/3` presentation renders payment, claim, deposit, and conditional
facility relations as a persistent map. `/4` renders only payment-mode
relations with non-negligible current model `flow`. Its stroke width and
opacity are both recalculated at the model's 24 Hz display cadence directly
from that flow: `min(7.5, 2.85 × flow^0.46)` and
`min(0.96, 0.68 × flow^0.3)`. There is no visible activation threshold or
minimum width. A relation is removed only below `0.002`, after its line is
already sub-pixel and effectively transparent. The same flow value is shown by
direct inspection; no potential facility or static outstanding claim is
substituted as a transaction.

The field retains `/3`'s C-VAL financial colour semantics without copying the
workstation chrome: ordinary settlement is warm off-white, public settlement
amber, and actual liquidity supply cyan. Red is reserved for a node's genuine
arrears, freeze stress, or default. A dried relation disappears; it does not
turn into a decorative red warning edge.

## Invariants

`4/model/` and its pure tests are standalone copies of `/3/model/` and retain
the `/1` actor counts, ledger, settlement ordering, liquidity lock, reference
economy, reset, keyboard operation, reduced-motion stepping, and fixed node
topology. Nodes remain present when they have no current transfers: the absence
of an edge means zero/insufficiently small current model flow, not the removal
of an actor or an invented failure.

Each actor's circle starts at the field's established radius. Its area
represents currently spendable cash relative to that actor's parallel unshocked
reference balance, so a liquidity lock immediately contracts the affected
circle to its actual 15% accessible balance; later receipts, expenditure, and
contagion are read as continuous expansion or contraction. Edge ports use the
same changing radii, so arrows always meet the visible balance circle rather
than its former outline.

The healthy baseline is deliberately dynamic rather than numerically frozen.
Firm, household, and fund payment groups each follow a small deterministic
operating rhythm with different 20-, 25-, or 40-period clocks. The rhythms are
zero-mean and their periods divide the long baseline check, so values visibly
vary while the unshocked system remains solvent, balanced, and recoverable.
For every unordered actor pair, the forward and reverse payment arrows occupy
separate cubic-Bezier corridors; the arrowhead scales from the same stroke
width as its transaction edge and is calculated directly from the curve's
terminal tangent rather than from an SVG marker. The model settles at 24 Hz, while the dense SVG
field samples geometry at 12 Hz. Flow labels use tabular numerals and a
six-phase, staggered half-second refresh: each value stays current without a
single full-field text-raster update.

Clicking a node locks 85% of its available cash. The lock changes subsequent
settlement through the model, but does not retain a filtered camera after the
pointer is released. Every live transaction edge displays its current model
flow to two decimal places; direct inspection additionally names the payer,
payee, and payment type.

## Open question

The threshold is a visual floor over the model's smoothed payment-rate signal,
not a claim of legal settlement finality. A later fork could test a different
flow horizon, but must retain the distinction between an active transfer and a
latent balance-sheet obligation.
