## Object and boundary

The object is not the proposition that all capitalism is a fraud or that every
rollover is “Ponzi finance.” That substitution would erase distinctions among
maturity transformation, sovereign issuance, productive assets, monetary
institutions, contracts, and legal regimes. The object is narrower and more
useful: a modern macro-financial economy is an interdependent payment system in
which obligations, deposits, refinancing, collateral values, and market access
must be renewed repeatedly.

In normal conditions, this recurrence permits wages, consumption, production,
tax collection, procurement, and investment. When multiple buffers are thin,
otherwise defensive actions—hoarding cash, cutting credit, selling collateral—
can reduce counterparties’ payments and refinancing capacity. The result can be
a positive feedback loop rather than a linear loss.

## The forest-level argument

### Stocks do not settle flows

A debt balance is not a payment. A firm may own collateral and have a loan, but
still fail to meet payroll today if receipts arrive late and refinancing is
withdrawn. A bank may have assets, but be unable or unwilling to extend cash at
the moment a counterparty needs to roll a position. A common collateral price
can change several balance sheets before a single legal default is recognized.

The relevant state is therefore not one aggregate-debt number or a count of
failed nodes. It is the timed relation between claims and the payment surface
that validates them.

### What “rollover” captures—and what it does not

Minsky distinguished hedge, speculative, and Ponzi finance by the relation
between income and debt-service commitments. The distinction is useful because
it asks how debt is validated by cash flow. It is not a license to label all
credit, government debt, or banking as a Ponzi scheme. A rollover relation is
fragile only under specified conditions: short commitments, insufficient liquid
buffers, impaired collateral, constrained lenders, or a loss of market access.

In this experiment, `rollover` names the conditional renewal of a maturing firm
loan. It is a mechanism, not a moral diagnosis.

### Why fragility is conditional rather than universal

Network density can share small shocks across more counterparties, yet broaden
exposure once a shock exceeds local capacity. The important variables are the
direction and weight of claims, common funding markets, maturity mismatch,
collateral constraints, and synchronized precautionary behaviour—not simply
the number of links.

Four mechanisms have to overlap for local stress to become a system event:

1. short payment or margin commitments are backed by long-lived or illiquid
   assets;
2. several actors depend on the same collateral, dealer, or short-term funder;
3. falling prices generate fresh liquidity, margin, or capital constraints;
4. rational cash preservation by one actor removes cash from another actor’s
   payment surface.

“One failure takes every head” is not a law of economics. It is a description of
how outcomes can become discontinuous near a threshold. This demonstrator must
show that conditionality rather than manufacture a universal collapse.

### Transmission to the real economy

The macro effect is not reducible to an employment counter. A payment and
refinancing contraction can jointly alter working capital, payroll, supplier
settlement, production timing, inventories, investment, prices, tax receipts,
market-making, and the availability of credit. A forced sale can mark down a
shared asset before any physical output changes; a missed commercial payment can
then turn that financial stress into a production interruption. Production and
financial networks are distinct networks that can amplify one another.

### Taleb, entropy, and energy: useful language with a boundary

A Taleb-style view shifts attention from an average quiet day to asymmetric,
hard-to-reverse tail losses. Thin liquidity buffers and heavy collateral reuse
can look efficient in ordinary time while carrying large conditional exposure
to a funding stop. This experiment does not estimate a tail distribution or
predict a black-swan event; it only makes amplification channels explicit.

Entropy and energy are not monetary equations. Financial claims cannot be
equated one-to-one with physical energy. The legitimate analogy is about slack:
complex payment systems require continuing information, trust, legal
enforcement, collateral valuation, and settlement infrastructure. Removing
redundancy can increase ordinary-time efficiency while reducing recovery room.
That is not a thermodynamic proof about markets.

## Synthetic model

The default `8 sectors` preset contains eight representative household sectors,
eight firms, four banks, four funds, one Treasury, and one central bank: 26
actors in total. It is the compact reading mode. The adjacent `20 sectors`
preset uses 200 households, 20 firms, and six banks. Each firm-facing household
sector is resolved into ten named households: cash, deposits, liquidity buffer,
wage, tax, and bank-income flow are each one tenth of the sector total. Each
household spends through a two-firm basket, so one household interruption
removes five percent—not ten percent—of either receiving firm's household
demand. Expanded consumption and representative tax rates are calibrated only
to eliminate deterministic cash leakage during a long normal observation; they
are not a real-world estimate. The original four funds, Treasury, central bank,
three procurement paths, and three interbank relations remain, yielding 232
actors. It keeps every actor named and each relation explicit; it is not a
visual zoom of an aggregate node. Firms and funding remain fixed central rings;
the 200 households occupy two stable peripheral fields, 100 at each side of the
institutional core. It contains the following ledger channels:

- wages, consumption, supplier settlement, tax, procurement, bank-income,
  repo, and bill rollover are `payment` relations;
- firm loans, deposits, and interbank credit are `claim` relations;
- interbank funding settlement makes bank liquidity a live propagation channel,
  rather than a decorative cross-link;
- the central-bank collateral window is a conditional `facility`, not a fake
  payment.

Payment demand has small relation-specific phase differences. The baseline is
therefore a circulating equilibrium, not a static diagram. A node shock reduces
available cash. As firm stress rises, an explicit precautionary-reserve rule
withholds some remaining cash; this creates arrears through payment constraints,
not through a selected-node-count trigger. Refinancing is constrained jointly by
the lender’s available liquidity and stress, the borrower’s collateral value,
and the remaining loan. Distressed firms and funds sell collateral; forced sales
lower the common collateral price; the lower price tightens refinancing again.

An actor fractures only after its modeled stress passes the stated threshold.
This is a synthetic stock-flow sketch, not a calibrated balance sheet, a legal
resolution model, a forecast, or a representation of any named country.

## Representation contract

The screen is a fixed financial map. Nodes never drift for visual atmosphere.
Change is visible only where it has a financial cause:

- every node has a permanent visible identity. The compact preset shows
  `H01`–`H08`, `F01`–`F08`, `BANK 01`–`BANK 04`, and `FUND 01`–`FUND 04`; the
  expanded preset extends the household series through `200`, the firm series
  through `20`, and the bank series through `06`; the original `FUND 01`–
  `FUND 04`, `TREASURY`, and `CENTRAL BANK` remain present;
- every node permanently displays rounded cash and its relevant balance-sheet
  value: firms show debt, banks show loan assets, funds show collateral assets,
  and households show cash and arrears;
- every claim relation is a persistent cubic Bezier arrow with a rounded
  outstanding balance and a layer-local `C` index. Payment relations have no
  permanent foreground graphic: each individual settlement appears as its own
  cubic Bezier arrow, carrying its rounded transaction amount and a `P` index,
  then vanishes until the next settlement;
- every relation has a graphite 0.3-opacity background rail in its active
  layer. This is the stable topology only: it has no value label and cannot be
  read as an actual settlement. A payment event is the only foreground edge,
  drawn over its rail with its sampled amount, label, and temporal sequence;
- the model uses a single nominal scale: one internal unit is displayed as ten
  million currency units (`M`). Consequently, an edge labelled `31M P04`
  carries a current settlement flow of 31 million, while `1280M C01` is a
  1.28-billion outstanding claim. The same scale is used for every node ledger;
- each payment relation has a deterministic but independently phased settlement
  window. Compact mode uses a 1.38–3.18 second cadence and 0.52–0.76 second
  window; the 1,102-payment expanded mode uses a 10.4–14.8 second cadence and
  a 0.36–0.48 second window. This keeps only a bounded foreground subset alive
  while still starting many independent settlements per second, without turning every
  label on at once. A payment has no foreground edge between events and never
  shares a global blink or dash phase with other relations. When its settlement
  window opens, the full directed curve is drawn once; its geometry and
  money-derived width remain fixed for the entire event. A cached dash phase
  traces that exact curve from payer to receiver, holds as a complete relation,
  then clears sequentially; the terminal arrow and its label arrive during the
  hold. Its width is a linear mapping of that sampled transaction's actual
  money amount, so a 100M settlement is twice the width of a 50M settlement
  above the minimum legibility floor; the current visual scale is uniformly
  doubled for both payment and claim edges. When a payment is constrained to
  zero, no payment edge appears. This is rendered directly on the single
  canvas, with no particle system or persistent full-screen trace buffer;
- relation codes, relation-layer membership, settlement cadence, screen-space
  Bézier paths, path lengths, arrow tangents, and node-ledger aggregation are
  cached or indexed. The payment topology has its own immutable offscreen
  canvas and redraws only on resize, preset reset, or layer change; claim rails,
  claim values, and node ledgers redraw at 8 Hz in the 200-household field.
  The foreground settlement trace uses a smooth presentation clock over those
  cached fields, while preserving the Retina backing store;
- red is an intermittent warning, not an immediate permanent state colour. A
  normal actor and relation remain graphite. The model derives an actor score
  from current stress, arrears relative to liquidity need, and an active
  selected shock; a payment relation inherits the greater endpoint risk and its
  own relative arrears. A material warning switches the node background every
  0.4 seconds, elevated risk every 0.2 seconds, and fracture/severe risk every
  0.1 seconds, and fracture/extreme risk every 0.05 seconds. The neutral
  0.3-opacity rail and every claim remain graphite; only an actively animating
  payment transmission and its label take the same red phase. The cadence
  communicates rising danger without reclassifying the whole topology as
  failed.

The layout is fixed because this experiment asks the participant to observe
changing financial relations against stable institutions. A moving topology
would make it harder to separate a shrinking payment edge from mere layout
motion.

The discipline comes from repository precedents rather than their skins:
Barabási–Albert/1 demonstrates a single clean field with a minimal control
margin; Three-body/2 demonstrates that traces and relation width must come from
measured state. Neither composition, palette, or geometry is copied.

## Bounded expansion record — 2026-09-10

The default compact map remains 26 actors and 121 relations (86 payment, 31
claim, 4 facility). The toggle creates a separate expanded state, rather than
mutating or scaling the compact state in place: it has 232 actors and 1,353
relations (1,102 payment, 247 claim, 4 facility). It retains the /2 model's
four fund-bank pairs, three procurement paths, and three interbank
funding/credit pairs. Each of the 20 household sectors is split into ten named
representatives, while the household stock and wage/bank-income totals remain
sector-conserving. The two-firm consumption basket reduces any one household's
firm-level exposure to five percent. A small deadband excludes ordinary
settlement residue from contagion pressure: a persistent high household shock
remains local across a 120-second pure-model observation, while a persistent
firm shock still crosses the refinancing/collateral threshold and propagates.

Changing presets resets time, stocks, shocks, selection, and relation caches,
because values cannot be carried coherently between different actor spaces. It
does not change the default mode's coordinates, actor count, relation IDs, or
settlement schedule. The fixed layout, single Canvas 2D renderer, geometry
cache, 20 Hz model tick, and separate topology/node/foreground paint paths
remain invariants. This is an observed behavior of the synthetic model, not a claim
about a real bank network.

The edge-motion rule transfers only the temporal sequence from
`goldfishes/screen/0908/overlay-2d-4`: an event is invisible through 11% of
its lifetime, traces from 16% to 56%, holds as a full path through 70%, and
clears sequentially thereafter. The arrow arrives from 50–57%; the label uses
the same arrival-to-clear interval. The financial adaptation keeps the full
Bezier path and its amount-derived width invariant, then advances one cached
dash phase across that fixed path. An earlier partial-path redraw was discarded
because it changed the visible edge form during an event and added per-frame
geometry work.
