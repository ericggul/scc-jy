## Object and boundary

The object is not the proposition that all capitalism is a fraud or that every
rollover is “Ponzi finance.” That substitution would erase distinctions among
maturity transformation, sovereign issuance, productive assets, monetary
institutions, contracts, and legal regimes. The object is narrower and more
useful: a modern macro-financial economy is an interdependent payment system in
which obligations, deposits, refinancing, collateral values, and market access
must be renewed repeatedly.

In normal conditions, this recurrence permits wages, consumption, production,
tax collection, procurement, and investment. When multiple buffers are thin,
otherwise defensive actions—hoarding cash, cutting credit, selling collateral—
can reduce counterparties’ payments and refinancing capacity. The result can be
a positive feedback loop rather than a linear loss.

## The forest-level argument

### Stocks do not settle flows

A debt balance is not a payment. A firm may own collateral and have a loan, but
still fail to meet payroll today if receipts arrive late and refinancing is
withdrawn. A bank may have assets, but be unable or unwilling to extend cash at
the moment a counterparty needs to roll a position. A common collateral price
can change several balance sheets before a single legal default is recognized.

The relevant state is therefore not one aggregate-debt number or a count of
failed nodes. It is the timed relation between claims and the payment surface
that validates them.

### What “rollover” captures—and what it does not

Minsky distinguished hedge, speculative, and Ponzi finance by the relation
between income and debt-service commitments. The distinction is useful because
it asks how debt is validated by cash flow. It is not a license to label all
credit, government debt, or banking as a Ponzi scheme. A rollover relation is
fragile only under specified conditions: short commitments, insufficient liquid
buffers, impaired collateral, constrained lenders, or a loss of market access.

In this experiment, `rollover` names the conditional renewal of a maturing firm
loan. It is a mechanism, not a moral diagnosis.

### Why fragility is conditional rather than universal

Network density can share small shocks across more counterparties, yet broaden
exposure once a shock exceeds local capacity. The important variables are the
direction and weight of claims, common funding markets, maturity mismatch,
collateral constraints, and synchronized precautionary behaviour—not simply
the number of links.

Four mechanisms have to overlap for local stress to become a system event:

1. short payment or margin commitments are backed by long-lived or illiquid
   assets;
2. several actors depend on the same collateral, dealer, or short-term funder;
3. falling prices generate fresh liquidity, margin, or capital constraints;
4. rational cash preservation by one actor removes cash from another actor’s
   payment surface.

“One failure takes every head” is not a law of economics. It is a description of
how outcomes can become discontinuous near a threshold. This demonstrator must
show that conditionality rather than manufacture a universal collapse.

### Transmission to the real economy

The macro effect is not reducible to an employment counter. A payment and
refinancing contraction can jointly alter working capital, payroll, supplier
settlement, production timing, inventories, investment, prices, tax receipts,
market-making, and the availability of credit. A forced sale can mark down a
shared asset before any physical output changes; a missed commercial payment can
then turn that financial stress into a production interruption. Production and
financial networks are distinct networks that can amplify one another.

### Taleb, entropy, and energy: useful language with a boundary

A Taleb-style view shifts attention from an average quiet day to asymmetric,
hard-to-reverse tail losses. Thin liquidity buffers and heavy collateral reuse
can look efficient in ordinary time while carrying large conditional exposure
to a funding stop. This experiment does not estimate a tail distribution or
predict a black-swan event; it only makes amplification channels explicit.

Entropy and energy are not monetary equations. Financial claims cannot be
equated one-to-one with physical energy. The legitimate analogy is about slack:
complex payment systems require continuing information, trust, legal
enforcement, collateral valuation, and settlement infrastructure. Removing
redundancy can increase ordinary-time efficiency while reducing recovery room.
That is not a thermodynamic proof about markets.

## Synthetic model

The network contains 20 households, 10 firms, four banks, four funds, one
Treasury, and one central bank. It contains six kinds of relation:

- wages, consumption, tax, procurement, bank-income, repo, and bill rollover
  are `payment` relations;
- firm loans and deposits are `claim` relations;
- the central-bank collateral window is a conditional `facility`, not a fake
  payment.

Payment demand has small relation-specific phase differences. The baseline is
therefore a circulating equilibrium, not a static diagram. A node shock reduces
available cash. As firm stress rises, an explicit precautionary-reserve rule
withholds some remaining cash; this creates arrears through payment constraints,
not through a selected-node-count trigger. Refinancing is constrained jointly by
the lender’s available liquidity and stress, the borrower’s collateral value,
and the remaining loan. Distressed firms and funds sell collateral; forced sales
lower the common collateral price; the lower price tightens refinancing again.

An actor fractures only after its modeled stress passes the stated threshold.
This is a synthetic stock-flow sketch, not a calibrated balance sheet, a legal
resolution model, a forecast, or a representation of any named country.

## Representation contract

The screen is a fixed financial map. Nodes never drift for visual atmosphere.
Change is visible only where it has a financial cause:

- every node has a permanent visible identity: `H01`–`H20`, `F01`–`F10`,
  `BANK 01`–`BANK 04`, `FUND 01`–`FUND 04`, `TREASURY`, and `CENTRAL BANK`;
- node geometry is a semantic identity system: household ledger cell, firm
  production cell, bank account chamber, fund market cell, Treasury bar, and
  central-bank settlement bar;
- every relation uses one solid black material and one directional rule; its
  only ordinary-state visual difference is modeled amount through width;
- short, high-contrast settlement strokes move along an edge only when that
  relation records an actual payment. These are not ambient particles or
  decorative dashes. Their speed, extent, and width derive from the relation’s
  current payment amount;
- the accumulated trace records already-settled payment paths. Red is reserved
  for arrears, pressure, and fracture after stress, never for category styling;
- a focused actor or relation reveals rounded integer cash, arrears, and
  relation amount. The permanent screen contains no decimal dashboard values.

The layout is fixed because this experiment asks the participant to observe
changing financial relations against stable institutions. A moving topology
would make it harder to separate a shrinking payment edge from mere layout
motion.

The discipline comes from repository precedents rather than their skins:
Barabási–Albert/1 demonstrates a single clean field with a minimal control
margin; Three-body/2 demonstrates that traces and relation width must come from
measured state. Neither composition, palette, or geometry is copied.

normal circulation with fluctuation but no autonomous fracture; recovery from a
brief local shock; and reduced remote wages, collateral price, and refinancing
