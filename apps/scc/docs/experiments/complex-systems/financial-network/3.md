# Financial network / 3 — live monetary field

Route: `/financial-network/3`.

## Tested relation

This is a presentation fork of `/financial-network/1`, not a new financial
model. It tests whether its fixed stock-flow ledger reads as one live monetary
field when the screen privileges changing payment capacity over a schematic of
actor categories.

The baseline is `/1`'s fixed institution map. The changed variable is the
graph field only:

- the initial view is the live payment field. Every 24 Hz model update changes
  a payment edge from its current `flow`; its visible stroke derives directly
  from that value. Claim view derives its stroke from current `principal`, and
  a collateral facility derives it from its current eligible limit. No
  displayed facility has a decorative fixed width;
- fixed cubic lanes keep opposite monetary obligations apart. Their arrowheads
  terminate outside the target node, so payer-to-payee and debtor-to-creditor
  orientation remains visible in the whole-field view;
- the screen uses the established C-VAL financial semantic palette without
  importing C-VAL's workstation panels: `#E6E5DD` is ordinary settlement,
  amber is public settlement and the active public institutions, cyan is
  liquidity/deposit/facility capacity, and red is reserved for arrears,
  freeze stress, and default. Actor sector is read through name, scale, and a
  restrained functional outline rather than six unrelated category colours;
- the full graph is the resting state. Relation kind and amount appear at a
  legible size only while its edge or an incident node is being inspected.
  Clicking to lock liquidity changes the model but does not retain graph
  isolation after pointer release.

## Invariants

`3/model/` and its pure tests are an independent copy of `/1/model/`.
Actor counts, initial ledger, settlement order, freeze rule, reference economy,
payment/debt views, keyboard operation, reduced-motion stepping, reset, and
panning/zooming retain `/1` behaviour. Nodes never drift: a visible change
still arises from the ledger's financial state.

Every relation retains its Korean kind and model amount through its direct
inspection label and accessible relation name. Selected actors still lock 85%
of available cash; red remains reserved for actual arrears, stress, and
default rather than a sector category. Releasing a lock never reconstructs
losses already recognised by the model.
