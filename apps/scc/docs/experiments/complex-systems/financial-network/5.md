# Financial network / 5 — adaptive transaction field

Route: `/financial-network/5`.

## Tested relation

This independent fork of `/financial-network/4` changes the economic model,
not its field language. It tests whether the same stock-flow payment system can
become a deterministic complex adaptive system: actors retain observations,
change their future rules from those observations, and alter payment and
deposit relationships. It remains a synthetic teaching model, not a calibrated
financial system or an empirical claim about bank or household behaviour.

## Adaptive mechanism

- Each bank accumulates an exponentially weighted memory of its own realised
  missed interest and principal payments. That memory tightens its future
  `lendingStandard`; no cash, loan principal, or capital is changed directly.
- A household records missed wage income and raises a bounded precautionary
  `savingPropensity`. It therefore requests less consumption from its own
  deposit balance until its lived income risk recedes.
- At deterministic one-second reviews, an unfrozen firm can compare the
  observed liquidity, loss memory and lending stance of alternative banks. A
  full deposit move is allowed only when the old bank can settle its matching
  reserve transfer; old loan claims remain with the old lender and any later
  credit forms a distinct new relation.
- Consumption and wage edges retain stable IDs but rewire their supplier or
  employer endpoint when that counterparty has defaulted or reached severe
  stress. Replacement choices are deterministic, use only viable firms, and
  favour more liquid, less-stressed, less-loaded candidates.

The model therefore has learning/memory, strategy change, and endogenous link
formation/dissolution. It is a deterministic, actor-network complex adaptive
financial-system simulation—not an empirical forecast and not a claim that
real actors use these exact rules.

## Retained field and accounting invariants

The `/4` transaction-only field remains intact: a line exists only for a
non-negligible current payment; its width and opacity derive directly from
current flow, while a dried flow disappears. Fixed node positions make a
rewired cubic curve visible as a changed economic connection rather than a
moving layout. The field still uses live amount labels, separate directed
cubic corridors, proportional tangent arrows, and the existing risk treatment.
The lower `노드명` control can hide all node-adjacent text for a less obstructed
field; it never hides payment amount labels.

Ordinary payments transfer existing deposits. Only firm credit creates a
matching deposit and loan; principal repayment extinguishes both. A deposit
bank switch moves the exact matching reserve asset between banks before its
`bankId` changes. The actor population and edge count do not change during
payment rewiring. The reference economy remains unshocked, so relative circle
area and risk comparisons stay meaningful.

## Verification

- `node --test apps/scc/components/complex-systems/financial-network/5/model/index.test.ts`
  - healthy 10,000-second baseline: no autonomous defaults, arrears, learned
    loss, saving response, deposit-bank move, or endpoint rewire;
  - a firm liquidity shock produces creditor loss memory, a tighter lending
    standard, and household precautionary saving; those memories later decay;
  - a frozen bank prompts a firm deposit-bank switch, while a defaulted firm
    prompts wage and consumption endpoint rewiring; all bank balance checks
    remain within `1e-7` and no actor or edge is fabricated.
- `pnpm --filter @scc/archive typecheck`

## Open question

Adaptation is currently deterministic and local. A later fork could test
heterogeneous expectations, bounded exploration, or selection among competing
bank strategies, but must preserve the stock-flow ledger and distinguish an
observed response from an empirical behavioural estimate.
