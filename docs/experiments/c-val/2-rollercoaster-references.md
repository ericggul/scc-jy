# C-VAL 2 rollercoaster — visual reference ledger

> Review date: 2026-08-06  
> Scope: a new Three.js scene for `/c-val/2/screen/rollercoaster` and
> `/c-val/2/screen/rollercoaster-legacy`.

This is a visual research ledger, not a licence to reproduce a ride, brand,
photograph, logo, passenger, setting, or lighting scheme. Sixty candidate
images were reviewed; the 43 pages below were retained because each gives one
specific, inspectable lesson about a real rail, wheel, train, camera, lighting,
or rendered coaster. The new scene takes only those structural lessons.

## New scene contract

1. **Participant situation:** a person looks at a surrounding screen after
   mobile V/A/L input has reached the market and become an executed price.
2. **Primary parameter:** the short, ordered execution-price history.
3. **Perceptual job:** read a change in price as a physically continuous rise,
   fall, or reversal—not as a chart line plus a themed illustration.
4. **Interaction job:** none here. The screen receives the shared snapshot and
   never writes market state.
5. **Wrapper justification:** an actual paired rail, sleepers, supports, and
   three wheel families turn the price path into a vehicle-bearing surface; the
   train is therefore constrained by the same object that represents price.
6. **System family:** the controller's `#20BD68` gain and `#E94A58` loss are
   used as the changing, high-chroma environmental field. The car, rails, and
   supports remain materially neutral so semantic colour does not become
   decoration.
7. **Removal test:** no city, people, text, logo, particle field, neon grid,
   dashboard element, independent vehicle lap, or decorative landscape is
   required for the price/rail/train relation and none is rendered.

## Engineering invariants

- Every input price is clamped for presentation to the real `$1–$100,000`
  installation universe before projection. A stable soft log-return transform
  keeps normal price movement visible and prevents any valid extreme from
  putting the rail beyond the camera.
- The complete 28-point rail always spans the available screen width. Its
  horizontal world span is recalculated from the viewport aspect rather than
  allowing a tall screen to crop the trajectory.
- The train has no autonomous loop. Each wheel assembly is positioned on the
  current curve, and price interpolation moves the entire rail and train
  together.
- Rails, sleepers, supports, and their geometry are allocated once. Animation
  updates existing typed buffers and instance matrices only; no timed geometry
  disposal/recreation occurs during the render loop.
- The renderer caps device pixel ratio at 1.5 and uses one 1024px soft-shadow
  pass. Reduced-motion preference applies price state without time
  interpolation.
